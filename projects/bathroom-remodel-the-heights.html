Bathroom remodel in The Heights

A tired blue-tile bathroom in a 1920s bungalow, rebuilt with a walk-in marble shower and a green vanity that suits the house.

Sample project. The photos are stock images showing the kind of work described, not this specific house. Replace them with real Kayman project photos before launch.

The house

A 1924 bungalow a few blocks off 19th Street, on its original pier-and-beam foundation. The only full bath had been redone in the 1970s with dark blue tile laid right over the old walls.

Why they called us

The owner noticed the floor flexing next to the tub, and the fan vented straight into the attic, where the decking had started to stain. She wanted a walk-in shower she could keep using as she got older, in a style that still fit a 100-year-old house.

The plan

Take the room down to the studs and joists, find out what was soft and why, then build it back properly: a walk-in shower with a continuous waterproofing membrane and a bench, marble walls, a painted vanity and brass fixtures that suit the period. A new fan, sized for the room, ducted outside through the roof.

What came up

Opening the floor showed decades of slow leaking from the tub drain. Two joists had rotted at the ends. We sistered them with new lumber, added blocking and laid a new subfloor before anything else. The work was all inside the house, so it didn’t need historic district review, but we kept the original window and trim profile anyway.

The result

Six weeks from demolition to the last coat of paint. The floor is solid, the shower has no curb to step over, and the attic stays dry.
